Strategy for a top leaderboard score

By the VectoFly team · Updated 11 November 2026

The world leaderboard keeps one best score per pilot. Reaching the top 50 takes more than reflexes. It takes consistent decisions about risk. These strategies assume you already know the basics from the beginner tips and How to play.

Think in terms of score per risk

Score = (distance + 5 × pills) × multiplier. Distance dominates: a long survival run is worth far more than any pill trail. Each pill is only 5 points, so a pill that costs you a hit is almost never worth taking. Your aim is not “collect everything”; it is “stay alive as long as possible while collecting the cheap pills”.

Choose the multiplier that suits you

A Hell run is worth twice an Easy run with the same distance. But a Hell run that ends at level 2 is worth less than a Hard run that reaches level 6. Play the level where you can reliably last a long time, then step up one level only when your results on the current one stop improving. The difficulty guide gives the numbers.

Read the director, not the obstacles

VectoFly’s obstacle director follows rules, and rules can be learned:

  • There is a minimum time between waves, so after you pass one you always have a short breathing space.
  • The gate of the next wall is never far from the previous one, so you rarely need a big move; a nudge is normal.
  • Spikes avoid pills and fuel cans, so a pill or can that is placed inside a safe zone is usually reachable.
  • Standing still calls a homing spike. Keep a gentle drift.
  • The director adapts to how you are doing, so very skilful flying is met with a steadier challenge, not a lucky break. Do not rely on luck.

Use the breathing space

The quiet moment after a wave is when you should do your housekeeping: glance at the fuel gauge, check the damage bar, pick up a pill that is on your line and re-centre your plane. Doing this during a wave is when mistakes happen.

Manage damage and fuel as resources

  • Damage: a damage bar near full means you can take almost no hit. Prioritise a pill, and fly more conservatively until it is repaired.
  • Fuel: fuel cans appear regularly, but a long gap can happen. Collect a can as soon as the gauge is under half, if it is safe to do so.
  • Do not let both resources run low at once. That is the usual end of a long run.

Plan pill trails

Pill trails often sweep up and down. Decide before the first pill whether the whole trail is safe: look past the first pills to where the last ones end. A trail that ends beside a wall is a trap. A magnet plane (Falcon, Saucer, Jetpack Hero) lets you collect pills with less movement, which keeps you in the safe middle of the screen.

Handle hazards by type

Each map’s hazards are a second layer on top of the spikes. For a record run, choose a map whose hazards you have practised. The hazards guide lists how each one behaves, and the maps page lists which map has which. A map with only birds and wind gusts is the most forgiving; maps with lasers, bombers and fighters demand more.

Choose the plane for the goal

  • Armor (Space Shuttle 50%, Royal Zeppelin and Sky Dragon 40%) lets you survive mistakes, which is the most direct way to a longer run.
  • Low fuel use reduces the pressure to hunt fuel cans.
  • Magnet keeps you near the centre while collecting pills.
  • Coin bonuses do not raise your score, so ignore them when you are only chasing the leaderboard.

Build a routine

  1. Warm up with two easier runs. Your first run of a session is usually your worst.
  2. Play at your best level in short blocks of 5 to 10 minutes.
  3. After a very good run, stop. Fatigue ends more records than difficulty does.
  4. Review a bad run: what ended it? A homing spike, a fuel problem or a hazard? Fix that one thing next time.

Play fair

The leaderboard is protected by signed run tickets and server-side checks, and cheats are rejected and removed. A score that you earned by flying is the only score that counts, and it feels better too. See how the board is protected.
